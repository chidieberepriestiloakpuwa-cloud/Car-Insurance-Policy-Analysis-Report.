### Car-Insurance-Policy-Analysis-Report.
##### Turning Car Insurance Data into Meaningful Insights for Smarter Business Decisions

#### About This Project
This project analyzes car insurance policy data to understand policyholder characteristics, claim amounts, claim frequency, vehicle preferences, and insurance usage patterns. Using Excel for data analysis and visualization, the project explores how factors such as age, marital status, education, gender, vehicle type, and car usage relate to insurance claims. The goal is to generate useful insights that can help insurance companies improve risk assessment, customer retention, pricing strategies, and claims management.

#### DATA SUMMARY:
The dataset contains 37,542 insurance records and 18 columns, covering policyholder demographics, vehicle information, coverage zones, household income, and insurance claims. The analysis examines customer distribution, claim amounts, average claim values, claim frequency, vehicle manufacturers, manufacturing years, and private versus commercial vehicle usage. The dashboard also provides a focused analysis of 7,514 policyholders in the Highly Urban coverage zone.

#### KEY FINDINGS:
1. Policyholder Distribution: The Highly Urban coverage zone contains 7,514 policyholders, with Single policyholders representing the largest marital-status group    at 3,122.
2. Claim Performance: The selected coverage zone recorded a total claim amount of $374,655,829.50, an average claim amount of $49,861.04, and a claim frequency of    3,881.
3. Vehicle Preferences: Ford is the most insured vehicle manufacturer, with 662 policyholders, followed by Chevrolet with 603.
4. Age and Claim Frequency: The 18–25 age group records the highest average claim frequency among the main age groups displayed, indicating a group that may          require 4. closer risk assessment.
5. Vehicle Usage and Gender: Private-use vehicles account for 79.99% of the claim amount, while commercial vehicles account for 20.01%. Male policyholders            represent approximately 50.44%, compared with 49.56% female policyholders.

#### RECOMMENDATION:
1. Adopt Risk-Based Pricing: Develop appropriate insurance pricing strategies based on customer demographics, vehicle characteristics, usage, and claim history.
2. Strengthen Claims Verification: Improve claim verification procedures and monitoring systems to identify suspicious claims and minimize fraudulent activities.
3. Introduce flexible insurance packages and loyalty rewards to improve customer retention and attract new policyholders.
4. Leverage data analytics to regularly review underwriting policies and support better business decisions.
5. Invest in safe-driving awareness programmes and incentive schemes to reduce claim frequency and improve profitability.

#### APPROACH:
The project began by exploring the insurance dataset and examining its structure, columns, and data types. The records were then reviewed for missing values and duplicate entries before analyzing policyholder demographics, vehicle characteristics, claim amounts, and claim frequency. Finally, the results were organized into charts, key performance indicators, and an interactive Excel dashboard to communicate findings and develop recommendations for improving insurance operations.

#### LIMITATIONS:
* Duplicate Policyholder ID: The dataset contains one duplicated ID associated with two different sets of policyholder information, which may affect accurate        policyholder identification.
* Limited Claims Information: The dataset does not provide detailed claim descriptions, causes of accidents, claim approval status, or claim settlement              information.
* Missing Policy Details: Information such as insurance premiums, coverage limits, policy types, and deductibles is unavailable, limiting deeper analysis of         insurance profitability and coverage performance.
* Limited Customer History: The dataset does not provide detailed historical policyholder records or driving behaviour, making it difficult to establish long-term   customer risk patterns.
* Data Quality and Scope: Some vehicle manufacturer names contain encoding inconsistencies, and the dashboard focuses on the Highly Urban coverage zone unless       another zone is selected. Therefore, its displayed figures should not be interpreted as totals for the entire dataset.
